## What we study

We study how animals and their microbiomes move nutrients, carbon, and microorganisms through ecosystems, how those transfers shape animal and human health, and how both change as environments become more variable.

**Kenya.** The Mara River and the Maasai Mara. Organic matter loading from hippos drives downstream hypoxia and fish kills when flushing flows arrive after long dry periods ([*Nature Communications* 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04391-6)). Hippo pools accumulate hippo gut microbes until the pool community resembles the gut rather than the river, a coupled system we named the meta-gut ([*Scientific Reports* 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-02349-1)). Mass drownings during the annual wildebeest migration feed the river for years afterward ([*PNAS* 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1614778114)).

**Democratic Republic of the Congo.** Maternal psychosocial stress is associated with reduced diversity in the early infant gut microbiome ([*Microorganisms* 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2607/11/4/975)), and the gut microbiome in early life predicts later malaria susceptibility ([*Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology* 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2026.1769376/full)).

**South Africa.** Kruger National Park. Artificial surface water broadens where and when herbivores use the landscape ([*Ecological Applications* 2026](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/eap.70185)), and water impoundments alter elephant distribution and activity in the southern park ([*African Journal of Ecology* 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/aje.70154)).

**Colombia.** The Magdalena River, where introduced hippos have established an expanding population. We model population growth under alternative management strategies and the coupled human and natural dynamics that follow.

**The Everglades.** Robotic rabbits for Burmese python control. We strip toy rabbits and rebuild them with motors, heaters, and solar power so they carry the movement and body temperature of a marsh rabbit, then place them in pens watched by a motion-triggered camera that signals a removal team when a python approaches. Live rabbits drew about one python a week but were too labor-intensive to keep in the field. The work is done with Robert McCleery's lab in UF Wildlife Ecology and Conservation and the South Florida Water Management District, and was covered by [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/burmese-pythons-robot-rabbits-florida-invasive-species/), [*The Washington Post*](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national/2025/08/28/pythons-everglades-florida-robot-rabbits/df3f630c-83c4-11f0-a8f4-28ba053d904b_story.html), and [*Smithsonian*](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/robo-bunnies-are-the-newest-weapon-in-the-fight-against-invasive-burmese-pythons-in-florida-180987018/) in 2025.

**Florida.** Carbon cycling across the terrestrial-aquatic interface of wetlands, alligators as ecosystem engineers linking wetland and riverine systems, and bottlenose dolphins as environmental sentinels for coastal health.

**Zoos and aquariums.** Health assessment for non-model species in human managed care. Nearly every animal in a zoo is a species with no reference gut microbiome, so there is no baseline against which a veterinarian can read a change. We build those baselines from feces, which requires no handling, no anesthesia, and no restraint, at institutions accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums. Extraction and full-length 16S nanopore sequencing run on the [lab's Opentrons OT-2 and Flex robots](/lab/), which is what makes sampling at this scale affordable. Sampled species range from great apes and gibbons to okapi, giraffe, rhinoceros, and hippopotamus, among many others. The lab holds research agreements with more than 20 US zoos and aquariums.

Our full research program and publication list are on the [Research](/research/) and [Publications](/publications/) pages.

## What gifts support

Federal grants fund specific projects on fixed timelines. They do not cover the costs below, or they cover them only for the period of a single award. Gifts to the WILDS fund are flexible and are used for:

**Undergraduate and graduate field participation.** Airfare, research permits, vaccination, and camp costs for a field season in Kenya. Gift funds cover these for students who cannot pay them.

**Sequencing and laboratory consumables.** Extraction kits, library preparation reagents, and flow cells are the recurring cost of every microbiome dataset we produce. Gift funds let us sequence pilot samples and follow unexpected results before a grant is written around them.

**Field operations in Kenya.** Our field camp and research operations in the Maasai Mara are run under contract by Soiyou Adventures, a Kenyan company. Gift funds pay for vehicle time, boat operation, and the sampling logistics that a research permit does not cover.

**Open-source environmental sensors.** We build and deploy low-cost sensors to record dissolved oxygen, temperature, and flow across our study sites. Gift funds cover hardware, replacement of units lost to floods and wildlife, and the deployment trips that maintain the record.

**Data archiving and open publication.** Article processing charges for open-access journals and long-term archiving of sequence data and code.

## Other ways to give

**Employer matching.** Many employers match charitable gifts. Check your employer's matching program and name the University of Florida Foundation as the recipient.

**Equipment and in-kind gifts.** We accept donated laboratory and field equipment where it fits our work. Contact us before shipping anything.

**Planned and major gifts.** For gifts requiring a written agreement, a named fund, or estate planning, UF Advancement staff in the College of Liberal Arts and Sciences handle the paperwork. Email us and we will connect you with them.
