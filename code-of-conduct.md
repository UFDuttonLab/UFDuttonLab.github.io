## Purpose and Status of This Document

This document sets out how the Dutton Lab works: what members can expect from the laboratory, what the laboratory expects from members, and what happens when something goes wrong. It covers everyone in the laboratory: the Principal Investigator and any other faculty, postdoctoral researchers, graduate students, undergraduate researchers, staff, visiting scholars, and field team members. Where a provision binds the PI, it binds the PI.

It is a **living document co-owned by the group**, not a directive from the Principal Investigator. Laboratory handbooks work best when drafted with the whole group, completed within a term rather than perfected indefinitely, revised on a schedule, and kept consistent with institutional policy. The documented benefits are concrete: less anxiety about unstated expectations, fewer misunderstandings, and an accountability pathway that exists before it is needed.<sup>[9](#ref9),[5](#ref5)</sup>

Expectations differ by career stage, and a single undifferentiated document serves no one well. The role-specific sections below are deliberately different from one another, and every one of them is **two-way**: it states what the mentee owes and what the mentor owes.<sup>[5](#ref5)</sup>

## Mentoring Philosophy

Mentorship is a working alliance built on trust and reciprocity rather than a transfer of instructions, and effective mentorship measurably improves retention, particularly for women, first-generation students, and students from historically underrepresented groups. The institutional context is worth naming honestly: fewer than half of undergraduate faculty believe their institution considers mentoring in promotion review, and only about a fifth of undergraduate science and engineering majors strongly agree that they had a mentor. Doing this well is a deliberate choice against the prevailing incentive.<sup>[1](#ref1)</sup>

The Principal Investigator's commitments to every member: regular scheduled meetings that are not cancelled by default; timely feedback on written work; honest assessment of progress, including when it is not going well; advocacy in the form of letters, introductions, and nominations; support for career paths outside academia without penalty; and mentor training on the PI's own part, including cultural humility and implicit bias.<sup>[1](#ref1),[8](#ref8)</sup>

## Expectations by Role

### Postdoctoral researchers

Development is structured against the six **National Postdoctoral Association Core Competencies**: discipline-specific conceptual knowledge, research skill development, communication skills, professionalism, leadership and management, and responsible conduct of research. We use those categories rather than inventing our own.<sup>[7](#ref7)</sup>

Postdocs maintain an Individual Development Plan, reviewed annually. UF's IDP requirement is written for PhD students, so for postdocs this is a lab expectation rather than a university one; the UF Graduate School templates work well enough for the purpose.<sup>[32](#ref32)</sup> They are expected to develop independent research directions, mentor junior members, and lead manuscripts. The laboratory commits to protected time for independent work, support for fellowship and faculty applications, and honest calibration of the job market. UF's Office of Postdoctoral Affairs coordinates appointment, benefits, visa policy, and professional development, and supports the UF Postdoctoral Association.<sup>[7](#ref7),[23](#ref23)</sup>

### Graduate students

Doctoral and master's students are expected to know their programme's regulations, which are their responsibility under UF Graduate School rules including supervisory committee requirements and the 3.00 GPA standard.<sup>[22](#ref22)</sup>

**UF requires an Individual Development Plan for every PhD student, and has since 2017.** It must be revised every year. Use the UF Graduate School templates: there is a Year 1 template and a separate one for Year 2 onwards. We review yours annually together. Master's students are not covered by the UF requirement but are welcome to use the same templates, and we recommend it.<sup>[32](#ref32)</sup>

UF points STEM students to myIDP as a supplementary tool. Separately, NIH encourages but does not require IDPs; what NIH requires is that the Research Performance Progress Report describe whether and how they are used, and the plans themselves are not submitted.<sup>[2](#ref2),[3](#ref3),[4](#ref4)</sup>

### Undergraduate researchers

Undergraduates receive high, clear, and realistic expectations covering weekly hours, expected duration of involvement, and dissemination goals, set in writing at the start. The intended arc runs from supportive duties toward genuine project ownership; students who stay long enough should end up owning something. **Every undergraduate completes responsible conduct of research instruction.**<sup>[8](#ref8)</sup>

### Field team members

Field operations in Kenya are run by **Soiyou Adventures**, whose staff are employed by that company under Kenyan law rather than by UF. That is an employment fact and not a statement about standing. They are colleagues: their credit is governed by the [Collaboration and Authorship Policy](/authorship/) on the same terms as anyone else, and the conduct expectations in this document govern how we work with them. Because UF's formal complaint channels are not open to non-UF personnel, the no-retaliation and conduct terms that actually bind sit in our contract with Soiyou, which is where they belong and where they should be kept current.

## Meetings and Communication

The laboratory holds a standing lab meeting and a journal club, and the PI holds individual meetings with each member. Cadence is set at the start of each semester and posted; members should expect these to be honoured rather than displaced by travel.

**There is no expectation of after-hours or weekend response.** Messages sent outside working hours do not carry an implied deadline, and members should not infer one. During field seasons, coverage and response expectations are set explicitly in the pre-departure meeting, because field seasons are where hour boundaries erode. Those expectations include a guaranteed rest day each week and uninterrupted sleep hours. Where a season would necessarily exceed an appointment's FTE cap, the reconciliation (comp time on return, a temporary FTE increase, or supplemental pay) is agreed with UF HR before departure rather than argued about afterwards.

## Hours, Leave, and Wellbeing

Graduate assistant workload is governed by the collective bargaining agreement between the UF Board of Trustees and Graduate Assistants United. **A 0.50 FTE appointment means an average of no more than 20 hours per week; 0.33 FTE means no more than 13.3 hours; 0.25 FTE means no more than 10 hours.** The agreement also provides five days of paid personal leave per semester appointment and six weeks of unpaid parental or medical leave per twelve months, with continued University health premium contributions and the right to return to the same or a similar position.<sup>[27](#ref27),[28](#ref28)</sup>

Members should note that the most recent agreement posted by UF Human Resources is the 2021 to 2023 contract and is nominally expired, with no successor posted. The laboratory treats those workload and leave terms as the operative floor regardless, and members should confirm current entitlements with UF HR or GAU.<sup>[27](#ref27)</sup>

Beyond the contractual floor: standard working hours, weekends, and vacation are respected; schedule autonomy is preferred over mandated hours; and wellbeing is treated as a prerequisite for good work rather than a reward for it. There is a documented mental health crisis in graduate education, and this laboratory's provisions exist because of it rather than in spite of it.<sup>[6](#ref6),[26](#ref26)</sup>

### Working in person

**Graduate students and postdoctoral researchers are expected to work in the laboratory rather than from home.** This is UF's position before it is ours. UF directed a full return to in-person work for employees on remote or hybrid arrangements in July 2025, with a deadline of 25 August 2025, and any exception requires **written approval from the Vice President of Human Resources** rather than an informal understanding with the PI.<sup>[30](#ref30),[31](#ref31)</sup>

Our own reason is narrower. The value of people being physically here is that questions get asked in passing, someone notices when a figure looks wrong before it reaches a manuscript, and junior members learn from overhearing conversations they were never going to think to request. A group that analyses its data separately at home is a group whose analysis goes unquestioned until peer review, which is late and expensive.

This is an expectation about where the work happens, not surveillance of your calendar. Fieldwork, conferences, teaching, illness, and travel are not working from home. A genuine remote arrangement goes through UF's process rather than around it. Where remote or hybrid work is sought as a **disability accommodation**, it goes to the UF ADA office through the interactive process described under Accommodations below, never through the PI's discretion, and nothing in this section narrows that route.<sup>[30](#ref30)</sup>

### Undergraduate researchers: terms

Undergraduate involvement is on one of three bases, stated in writing before starting: paid hourly, course credit, or volunteer. Whichever applies, a maximum weekly hours figure is agreed at the outset, hours above it are neither expected nor a condition of a letter of recommendation, exam periods and illness reduce hours without penalty, and no undergraduate is asked to incur an unreimbursed cost.

### Accommodations

Any member may request an adjustment to laboratory or field work. Requests go through UF's Disability Resource Center or ADA coordinator rather than being decided informally in the laboratory, and may be raised with the Department Chair rather than the PI. Where a physical requirement exists, such as the swim-competency check in the [Field Safety and Travel Policy](/field-safety/), field roles are redesigned around the requirement rather than the member being excluded; failing a swim check does not end field participation, and non-water roles are available in every season.

## Routes Outside the Laboratory

Several policies route a decision or a contact to the **Department Chair** because the Principal Investigator cannot fairly decide a matter in which they are a party. The Chair holds no obligation to this laboratory by virtue of our writing it down. The PI is responsible for confirming with the Chair, annually, that these routes are accepted, and for naming the current Chair and an alternate in the laboratory onboarding pack. Where the Chair declines or does not respond within ten working days, the matter goes to the College Dean. Harassment matters go to the Title IX Office rather than to the Ombuds, for the reason given below.

## Culture and Psychological Safety

The organising concept here is **psychological safety**: a shared belief that the team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. It is a measured construct that predicts team learning behaviour and, through it, performance. It is the reason "tell me when you break something" is a productivity policy and not merely a kindness.<sup>[11](#ref11)</sup>

In practice this means: mistakes and failed experiments are reported early and without penalty; rejection and failure are discussed openly rather than hidden; wins are celebrated; and disagreement with the PI is expected and protected. If you think an analysis is wrong, say so, including when it is the PI's analysis.<sup>[6](#ref6),[10](#ref10)</sup>

Members hold named, expected roles in maintaining this culture, not only the PI. These include onboarding help for new members, skill-sharing sessions, peer mentoring, and watching out for colleagues' wellbeing.<sup>[10](#ref10)</sup>

We follow the Ecological Society of America Code of Ethics, whose provisions on general conduct, professional practice, and publication apply to everything this laboratory does, including its obligations to minimise harm to ecosystems and to engage meaningfully with local communities.<sup>[12](#ref12)</sup>

## Harassment, Discrimination, and Reporting

The single most important finding in this area is structural: **organizational climate is the strongest predictor of sexual harassment, and legal-compliance-driven training does not reduce it.** This section is therefore written as climate work rather than as compliance documentation.<sup>[13](#ref13)</sup>

Field sites are the highest-risk setting in this laboratory's portfolio, and fewer than half of surveyed researchers recalled ever encountering a code of conduct at a field site. The evidence and the resulting controls are set out in the [Field Safety and Travel Policy](/field-safety/), which requires a signed field code of conduct before every deployment.<sup>[15](#ref15)</sup>

**Protected categories.** UF Regulation 1.006 prohibits discrimination and harassment on the basis of race, creed, colour, religion, age, disability, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity and expression, marital status, national origin, political opinions or affiliations, genetic information, and veteran status.<sup>[19](#ref19)</sup>

**Before you choose whom to talk to, know who is obliged to report.** Under UF Policy 5-003, **every UF employee** who receives information about, observes, or suspects prohibited conduct must promptly report it to the Title IX Office. UF operates no "responsible employee" tier, so the Principal Investigator, laboratory staff, and graduate assistants are all covered. If you tell any of us, we are required to pass it on. That is UF's rule rather than ours and we cannot waive it, which is exactly why you should know it before deciding where to take something. You are not required to report conduct you experienced yourself.<sup>[29](#ref29)</sup>

**The Ombuds is not a confidential route for harassment.** UF's Ombuds holds communications in confidence except where there is imminent risk of serious harm **or in cases involving sexual harassment**. It is a genuinely useful independent route for supervision disputes, workload, and interpersonal conflict. It is not confidential for the category most likely to send someone there.<sup>[18](#ref18),[14](#ref14)</sup>

**Confidential support** comes from UF's designated Confidential Resources, defined in Policy 5-003 as employees whose official responsibilities include providing mental health counselling and who are not obliged to report to the Title IX Coordinator.<sup>[29](#ref29)</sup>

**Formal routes**, none of which require you to raise the matter with the PI first: the UF Title IX Office (titleix@ufl.edu, 352-273-1400, report.ufl.edu); UF Regulation 1.0063 for academic personnel and staff; UF Regulation 4.012 for student grievances, which carries short appeal deadlines worth checking at the outset; and, for graduate assistants, the grievance article of the collective bargaining agreement.<sup>[17](#ref17),[20](#ref20),[21](#ref21),[27](#ref27)</sup>

Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern, in any of these channels or informally within the laboratory, is itself a violation of this policy.

## Research Integrity

UF defines research misconduct as **fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism** in proposing, performing, or reviewing research, or in reporting research results, requiring a significant departure from accepted practices and proved by a preponderance of the evidence.<sup>[24](#ref24)</sup>

**All faculty, staff, and students must promptly report observed or suspected research misconduct.** The named route is the UF Research Integrity Officer, Cassandra C. Farley, or Deputy Research Integrity Officer, Amber Moore, at RIO@research.ufl.edu or 352-392-9174. An anonymous alternative is the UF Compliance Hotline at 877-556-5356.<sup>[24](#ref24),[25](#ref25)</sup>

Because much of this laboratory's work is computational, findings of falsification in practice turn on records. Notebook discipline, version control, and data retention under the [Data and Code Management Policy](/data-management/) are integrity infrastructure, not housekeeping.

## Authorship and Credit

Authorship is governed by the [Collaboration and Authorship Policy](/authorship/) and awarded on actual contribution. Consistent with ESA publication standards: authorship requires substantial contribution; the author list is not changed without the consent of those affected; all coauthors approve the final manuscript; work is not submitted to more than one journal at a time; and post-submission errors are corrected promptly.<sup>[12](#ref12),[6](#ref6)</sup>

Effort in this laboratory is tracked through the equipment scheduling application, in part so that contribution can be credited accurately rather than remembered selectively. Those records are one input among several and are never the sole basis for a contribution determination, since they capture bench time on Gainesville instruments and are structurally blind to analysis, writing, study design, and all fieldwork. They are not used for employment, hours, or performance purposes.

## International Collaboration

Members working on the Kenya programme are expected to internalise equitable partnership practice early rather than learning it late: co-developed research agendas, authorship for local intellectual contributions, use of local infrastructure and local sample processing where feasible, local specimen deposition, compliance with local law and permitting, an explicit capacity-building component, and dissemination back to local audiences in open access. These are auditable commitments, set out in full in the [International Research Partnership Policy](/international-partnership/).<sup>[16](#ref16),[12](#ref12)</sup>

## Onboarding and Departure

New members receive this document, the other laboratory policies, a safety orientation, and an assigned peer contact in their first week. Departing members complete the offboarding checklist in the [Data and Code Management Policy](/data-management/), covering data custody, repository transfer, and notebook disposition, and the authorship provisions for departing authors in the [Collaboration and Authorship Policy](/authorship/).

## Review

This document is reviewed annually with the whole laboratory, and amended by agreement rather than by unilateral revision.
