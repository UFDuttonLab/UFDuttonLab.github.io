Key takeaways
  1. Ne is usually smaller than the head count. Animals that never breed add to the census but not to the gene pool.
  2. The rarer sex sets the limit. With 1 breeding male, Ne can never reach 4, no matter how many females breed.
  3. An equal sex ratio gives the largest Ne for a fixed number of breeders. Compare 8 males and 8 females with 14 males and 2 females.
  4. Small Ne means fast genetic drift. Alleles are lost by chance, so the population has less variation to draw on when climate, habitat or disease changes.
Ne = 4 Nm Nf / (Nm + Nf) for unequal numbers of breeding males and females (Wright 1931, Genetics 16:97). Expected heterozygosity after t generations is (1 − 1/(2Ne))t of the starting value. Drift lines are Wright-Fisher simulations with 2Ne gene copies.
