Key takeaways
  1. Temperature is a primary driver. Microbial decomposition roughly doubles for every 10°C increase (Q₁₀ ≈ 2), so tropical litter is half gone within months while tundra litter takes about 6 years.
  2. Moisture enables microbial activity. Decomposers need water. In deserts microbes are water limited despite high temperatures, and sunlight (photodegradation) breaks down much of the surface litter instead.
  3. Too much water slows decomposition. Waterlogged soils become anaerobic, which suppresses aerobic decomposers. Peat bogs preserve organic matter for thousands of years.
  4. This explains soil carbon patterns. Tropical forests have thin organic layers because decomposition outpaces litter input. Boreal forests and peatlands build deep organic soils because litter input outpaces decomposition.
Model: k = 1.9 × 2(T−25)/10 × f(moisture) + photodegradation. Preset k values fall within litterbag ranges from Parton et al. 2007, Science 315:361. Photodegradation after Austin and Vivanco 2006, Nature 442:555.
