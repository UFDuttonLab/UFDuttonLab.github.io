Key takeaways
  1. Sources export, sinks import. In a source, births exceed deaths (r above 0), so surplus animals disperse. In a sink, deaths exceed births (r below 0), and the population lasts only while immigrants keep arriving.
  2. Protect the sources. Keep populations 1 and 4 and the metapopulation persists. Keep only the two sinks and both go extinct, usually within 150 to 250 years.
  3. Open versus closed populations. Set dispersal to 0 with nothing protected. Each population now runs on births and deaths alone (the BD model), and the sinks go extinct. Dispersal is the I and E in BIDE.
  4. A crowded patch is not always a good patch. A sink can hold many animals while losing more than it produces. Protecting poor habitat by mistake risks an ecological trap.
The model follows individual animals and includes chance, so no two runs are identical. Each year every animal has a 10% chance of dying. Its chance of leaving one offspring is 10% + r(1 − N/K) where r is above 0 and 10% + r where r is below 0, with K = 500, so the expected yearly change is rN(1 − N/K) or rN. Each animal then leaves with the dispersal probability and settles in one of the other surviving populations at random. Source-sink dynamics follow Pulliam 1988, American Naturalist 132:652.
