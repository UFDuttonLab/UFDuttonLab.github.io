Key takeaways
  1. Burning moves carbon; it does not destroy it. Every tonne taken from underground ends up in the air or the ocean. The number of dots never changes.
  2. The ocean absorbs CO₂, but slowly. It keeps taking up carbon for centuries, chasing a balance with the air, even after the burning stops.
  3. The balance is a new normal. Once all the fossil carbon is out and the ocean catches up, CO₂ stays far above where it started. Roughly a quarter to a third of what we burned stays in the air.
  4. The ocean pays for it. The carbon the ocean absorbs makes seawater more acidic, and the pH keeps falling after emissions stop. Only over tens of thousands of years do dissolving seafloor carbonate and rock weathering bring CO₂ back down.
Simplified two-box model (air and ocean); plants and soils are left out. The ocean takes up CO₂ in proportion to the gap between the air and the ocean’s own balance point, which rises as the ocean fills (buffering).
